Accessibility

Travel constraints are part of the decision.

Trip planning should consider walking distance, accessible stops or vehicles, transfers, and route limitations. The prototype provides space for these needs, but cannot verify whether a real-world route is accessible.

Before traveling, confirm accessibility and service details with the relevant transportation provider. Example results on this page are not validated travel guidance.

Information architecture

Designed around the trip, not the transport directory.

Content organization: Home, Plan a Trip, Compare Options, Accessibility, Travel Guidance, and About form the task-oriented structure. Walking, Biking, Public Transit, and Shared Transportation are comparison dimensions.

Navigation: Users can follow Trip Details → Preferences → Compare Options → Trip Summary, return to previous steps, and edit choices.

Labels: Familiar action-oriented terms such as Plan a Trip, Compare Options, Edit Trip, Cost, Estimated Time, Walking Distance, and Accessibility support recognition.

User pathways: Michael prioritizes reliable travel time, Sophia cost, Danielle convenience, and David clear directions. All begin with the same trip definition and then compare the options relevant to them.

Academic concept: The proposal calls for user interviews, card sorting, tree testing, and task-based usability testing. The current page is a sample interface, not evidence that those studies have already occurred.
